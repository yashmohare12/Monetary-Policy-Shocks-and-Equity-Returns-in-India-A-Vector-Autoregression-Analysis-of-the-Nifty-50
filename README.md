# Monetary-Policy-Shocks-and-Equity-Returns-in-India-A-Vector-Autoregression-Analysis-of-the-Nifty-50


his paper examines how equity returns in India respond to monetary policy shocks. Using
202 monthly observations from January 2010 to October 2026, we estimate a four-variable
vector autoregression (VAR) in changes in the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) repo rate, Brent
crude oil returns, USD/INR exchange-rate returns and Nifty 50 returns. All series are sta-
tionary after transformation, and a VAR(2) is selected by the Akaike information criterion.
Orthogonalised impulse responses show that a one-standard-deviation repo shock (about 15
basis points) is associated with a same-month Nifty response of +0.68 percentage points,
which is only marginally significant, and that all later responses are statistically indistin-
guishable from zero. Granger-causality tests and the forecast-error variance decomposition
tell the same story: repo changes explain about 2% of Nifty variance. Residual diagnos-
tics support the specification, although return residuals display heavy tails driven by the
March 2020 collapse. We conclude that, at monthly frequency, there is little robust evidence
that repo-rate surprises move Indian equity returns, and we discuss why this is likely to
reflect anticipation and the infrequency of policy changes rather than a literal absence of
transmission.
Keywords: monetary policy, repo rate, Nifty 50, VAR, impulse response function
